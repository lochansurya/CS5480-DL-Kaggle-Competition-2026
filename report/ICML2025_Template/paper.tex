\begin{abstract}
We present a custom deep convolutional neural network for binary image
classification built entirely from scratch in PyTorch, without any
pretrained weights.  The architecture combines a ResNet-34-like backbone
with Convolutional Block Attention Modules (CBAM) after every residual
block and Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling for richer global descriptors.
A key contribution is the \textbf{ShapePreprocess} pipeline: each input
image is converted to a grayscale edge map
(RGB $\to$ Grayscale $\to$ Contrast$\times$3 $\to$ \texttt{FIND\_EDGES}
$\to$ RGB) before entering the network.  This deterministic transform
directly exposes the discriminative shape signal --- smooth elliptical
outlines for spheres versus sharp rectangular corners for cubes --- and
eliminates colour as a spurious feature.  Combined with Test-Time
Augmentation (horizontal flip), threshold calibration on the validation
set, and light regularisation, the system achieves a public leaderboard
score of \textbf{0.775311} (77.53\%) on the IITH Deep Learning
Hackathon 2026 (CS5480), an improvement of +1.9\% over the previous
best of 0.755860.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Binary image classification is a foundational problem in computer
vision.  The IITH Deep Learning Hackathon 2026 poses a restricted
variant of this problem: all models must be trained \emph{from scratch}
on a provided dataset of labelled images; pretrained weights from any
external source are prohibited.  This constraint forces the model to
extract useful representations purely from the supplied data, without
the benefit of large-scale pretraining.

Through careful visual inspection of the training images we determined
that the dataset consists of \textbf{CLEVR-style} rendered 3-D
scenes~\cite{johnson2017clevr}: each image depicts exactly four small
geometric objects (cubes, cylinders, and spheres) placed on a plain
grey background.  The binary label encodes a simple rule:

\begin{center}
\textbf{Class 1}: scene contains at least one \emph{sphere} \textbf{AND}
at least one \emph{cube}. \\
\textbf{Class 0}: all other configurations.
\end{center}

This insight is crucial: the discriminative signal is \textbf{purely
shape}.  Sphere edges form smooth elliptical contours; cube edges form
sharp rectangular corners.  Colour, shading, and background are
identical across classes and provide no classification signal.

Two practical challenges followed from this analysis:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Destructive~augmentations.}  \texttt{RandomResizedCrop}
  can exclude the only sphere or cube from the crop window, silently
  mislabelling the sample on every training iteration.
\item \textbf{Colour bias.}  Standard RGB input tempts the model to
  exploit spurious colour cues, wasting capacity and limiting
  generalisation.
\end{enumerate}

Our solution --- the \texttt{ShapePreprocess} edge-map transform
combined with a clean training pipeline --- addresses both issues and
yields the current best score of 0.775311.

Training images are split 85\%/15\% for training and validation using
a random permutation with fixed seed 42 (\texttt{split\_seed=42},
\texttt{torch.randperm}), ensuring reproducibility and zero leakage.

\subsection{ShapePreprocess: Edge-Map Preprocessing}
\label{sec:preprocess}

The key preprocessing step converts each RGB image into a shape outline
map before any network processing:
\begin{align*}
  &\text{RGB} \to \text{Grayscale (L)} \to \text{Contrast}{\times}3 \\
  &\quad\to \texttt{FIND\_EDGES} \to \text{RGB (3 identical channels)}
\end{align*}

\textbf{Why this works.}  In the original RGB images, the classification
signal (sphere or cube present?) is embedded in the shading of small
object surfaces.  The edge-map transform amplifies the only true signal:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A \textbf{sphere} projects as a smooth, nearly circular/elliptical
    contour with no corners.
  \item A \textbf{cube} projects as a compact set of straight edges meeting
    at sharp corners.
  \item A \textbf{cylinder} has a rounded top-cap and two vertical edges.
\end{itemize}
After the transform, the network receives near-binary outline images
whose geometry directly encodes the class label.  Colour, shading, and
background intensity are discarded entirely, preventing the model from
learning spurious correlations.

Normalisation is changed from ImageNet statistics to
$\mu = \sigma = [0.5, 0.5, 0.5]$, appropriate for near-binary edge
images.

\subsection{Training Augmentation}

Only shape-preserving augmentations are applied during training:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ShapePreprocess} (deterministic) --- applied first.
  \item \textbf{Resize} to $224\!\times\!224$ --- all four objects remain
    visible; \texttt{RandomResizedCrop} is deliberately omitted to prevent
    label corruption.
  \item \textbf{RandomHorizontalFlip} ($p=0.5$) --- edge outlines are
    flip-symmetric.
\end{itemize}
Colour jitter, Gaussian blur, random erasing, MixUp, and CutMix are
all \emph{disabled}: they destroy the high-frequency edge signal that
is the sole discriminative feature.

\subsection{Backbone Architecture}

$(3,4,6,3)$~\cite{He2016}, totalling 16 residual blocks across four
stages with channel widths $\{64, 128, 256, 512\}$.  A
$7\!\times\!7$ convolutional stem (stride~2) followed by
$3\!\times\!3$ max-pooling (stride~2) reduces the spatial resolution
from $224\!\times\!224$ to $56\!\times\!56$ before the residual
stages begin.  The full architecture is summarised in

\subsection{BasicBlock with CBAM and DropPath}

Each \textbf{BasicBlock} executes the following forward pass:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Conv1}: $3\!\times\!3$ Conv -- BN -- ReLU.
  \item \textbf{Conv2}: $3\!\times\!3$ Conv -- BN \emph{(no ReLU)}.
  \item \textbf{CBAM} attention applied to the Conv2 output.
  \item \textbf{DropPath} on the full residual branch.
  \item Add shortcut (with $1\!\times\!1$ Conv + BN projection when
        input/output dimensions differ), then apply \textbf{ReLU}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)}

CBAM~\cite{Woo2018} refines each feature map through two sequential
gates.

\textbf{Channel Attention.} Global average-pooled and max-pooled
descriptors are each passed through a shared two-layer MLP (reduction
ratio 16).  Their outputs are summed and sigmoid-gated:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{w}_c = \sigma\!\bigl(
    \mathrm{MLP}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{avg}})
    + \mathrm{MLP}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{max}})\bigr).
\end{equation}

\textbf{Spatial Attention.} Channel-wise average and maximum
projections are concatenated and passed through a $7\!\times\!7$
convolution followed by a sigmoid:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{w}_s = \sigma\!\bigl(
    \mathrm{Conv}_{7\times 7}(
    [\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{avg}};\,\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{max}}])\bigr).
\end{equation}

The final attended feature map is
$\mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{w}_s \odot (\mathbf{w}_c \odot \mathbf{x})$.

\subsection{Generalized Mean (GeM) Pooling}

GeM Pooling~\cite{Radenovic2018} generalises average pooling with a
learnable power parameter~$p$ (initialised to 3):
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{f} = \Bigl(\frac{1}{HW}
    \sum_{h,w} x_{h,w}^{\,p}\Bigr)^{1/p}.
\end{equation}
Larger values of $p$ place greater emphasis on the most strongly
activated map positions, yielding descriptors more sensitive to salient
object features than standard mean pooling.  We retain GeM rather than
concatenated average+max pooling (ConcatPool2d), as the latter was
found to add noise without benefit on this dataset.

\subsection{Stochastic Depth (DropPath)}

Stochastic Depth~\cite{Huang2016} is applied across all 16 residual
blocks.  Drop probabilities are evenly spaced from 0 (first block) to
$p_{\max} = 0.1$ (last block) via \texttt{torch.linspace}:
\begin{equation}
  p_l = \frac{l-1}{L-1}\cdot p_{\max}, \quad l = 1,\ldots,L=16.
\end{equation}
The first block is never dropped ($p_1=0$); drop probability increases
linearly to $p_{16}=0.1$.  During training, entire residual branches
are randomly dropped and the surviving branch is rescaled by
$1/(1-p_l)$.

\subsection{Classifier Head}

The 512-d GeM descriptor feeds a two-layer MLP head:
\begin{align*}
  &\mathrm{Dropout}(0.2) \to \mathrm{Linear}(512, 256) \to \mathrm{ReLU} \\
  &\quad\to \mathrm{Dropout}(0.1) \to \mathrm{Linear}(256, 1).
\end{align*}
A single logit is produced and thresholded after sigmoid activation.

\section{Training Strategy}
\label{sec:training}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Optimiser and Learning Rate Schedule}

AdamW~\cite{Loshchilov2019} is used with learning rate
$\eta_0 = 7\!\times\!10^{-4}$ and weight decay $\lambda = 10^{-4}$.
The schedule uses 5 linear warm-up epochs followed by cosine annealing:
\begin{equation}
  \eta(t) = \begin{cases}
    \eta_0 \cdot \dfrac{t+1}{T_w} & t < T_w, \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{\eta_0}{2}\!\Bigl(1 + \cos\dfrac{\pi(t-T_w)}{T - T_w}\Bigr)
      & t \ge T_w,
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $T_w = 5$ warm-up epochs and $T = 100$ is the maximum epoch
budget.

\subsection{Loss Function}

Binary cross-entropy with logits is used with label smoothing
\emph{disabled} ($\varepsilon = 0.0$).  Hard binary targets are
appropriate for a well-separated two-class problem on synthetic data.
Label smoothing was tested and found to hurt performance.

\subsection{Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)}

At both validation and test time, each image is forward-passed twice:
once normally and once horizontally flipped.  The two sigmoid outputs
are averaged before thresholding.  TTA provides a consistent
$\approx 0.5\%$ improvement at near-zero computational cost.

\subsection{Two-Phase Training Procedure}

The pipeline runs two sequential phases per random seed:

\textbf{Phase~1 --- Validation training.}  The model is trained on the
85\% training split with early stopping (patience~20).  After
convergence, TTA probabilities are collected on the 15\% held-out split
and the optimal threshold is computed via grid search
(Section~\ref{sec:training}).

\textbf{Phase~2 --- Full retrain.}  A fresh model with the same seed
is trained on the \emph{entire} training set for the same epoch budget
(100 epochs, no early stopping since there is no validation split).
Test-set predictions from this model are thresholded at the
Phase~1-derived threshold.

This two-phase design ensures that the decision boundary is calibrated
on held-out data while the final model benefits from all available
labels.

\subsection{Threshold Optimisation}

Rather than fixing the classification threshold at 0.5, we perform a
fine-grained search over 401 evenly spaced values in $[0.05, 0.95]$
on the held-out validation set and select the threshold that maximises
accuracy.  Figure~\ref{fig:threshold} shows the full accuracy-vs-threshold
curve for the final run; the optimal threshold was found well below 0.5,
consistent with the model output distribution being skewed by the
edge-map input distribution.

\subsection{Key Observations}

\textbf{RandomResizedCrop causes label corruption.}
In CLEVR-style scenes with four small objects, a random crop can
exclude the only sphere or cube from the frame, silently mislabelling
the sample.  Replacing this augmentation with a fixed \texttt{Resize}
removed a source of systematic noise on every training step.

\textbf{Colour is a spurious feature.}
The same set of colours (red, blue, green, purple, grey, brown, cyan)
appears in both Class~0 and Class~1 scenes.  Any model using colour
information wastes capacity and introduces non-generalising correlations.
The edge-map transform eliminates colour entirely by design.

\textbf{Heavy augmentation hurts on synthetic crisp data.}
MixUp ($\alpha = 0.8$) and CutMix ($p = 0.5$) both damaged the
sharp geometric edges that carry the sole classification signal.
Label smoothing penalised high-confidence predictions on a task where
the class boundary is unambiguous.

\textbf{TTA provides a consistent gain.}
Averaging predictions over the original image and its horizontal flip
improves accuracy by approximately 0.5\% with no training overhead.

\textbf{Threshold calibration is essential.}
The optimal decision threshold (Figure~\ref{fig:threshold}) differs
substantially from 0.5 for edge-map inputs.  Fixing the threshold
at 0.5 would sacrifice several percentage points.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

We presented a binary image classification pipeline trained entirely
from scratch using a ResNet-34-like backbone enhanced with CBAM
dual-attention, Generalized Mean Pooling, and Stochastic Depth.  The
key contribution is the \textbf{ShapePreprocess} edge-map transform
that converts every input image into a grayscale shape outline map,
directly encoding the sphere-vs-cube discrimination signal.  Combined
with a clean training pipeline (no destructive augmentation, TTA,
threshold calibration), the system achieves a public leaderboard score
of \textbf{0.775311} on the IITH Deep Learning Hackathon 2026 (CS5480).

The most significant remaining challenge is the gap between strong
validation accuracy and the leaderboard score, which reflects the
difficulty of generalising to unseen scene configurations from a
finite synthetic dataset without pretrained initialisation.
