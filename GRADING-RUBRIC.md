Hackathon Grading Rubric
Rubric
Dear students,
 
First, congratulations on giving great attempts at solving the hackathon! A couple of you might have potentially contributed significantly to an open research problem which is very impressive. If indeed your score remains valid after the TAs' sanitary checks, you are highly encouraged to come and explore this research direction. 
 
That being said, we would like to clearly state the planned grading rubric for this hackathon, as the rubric stated on the Kaggle page might have been a little ambiguous. The rubric is as follows:
 
1. If you are not part of a team that participated in the hackathon, you will be directly awarded zero, irrespective of anything else you submit for this assessment.


2. If it is inferred that you used a pre-trained model for your final submission to the hackathon, you will be directly awarded a zero, and your rank on the leaderboard will not be considered when evaluating other legitimately participating teams.


3. Your normalized rank on the private leaderboard, compared against all participating teams, contributes 40% to your score for this assessment.


4. Your normalized rank on the public leaderboard contributes 20% to your score for this assessment.


5. Your submitted code being reproducible upto 3% error as compared to your final scores for both the public and private test datasets contributes 10% to your total score.


6. Your report contributes 30% to your total score, with the following fine-grained rubric:

6a. Your observations about the dataset, what it consists of, and what about it makes it hard or easy to learn an underlying set of classification rule(s) contributes 8% (8% of the total score, not 5% of this 30%!).

6b. The quality of your final approach and the clarity with which it is presented contributes 10%. Do not hesitate to add figures or other kinds of information that may aid in easily understanding your solution. 

6c. What approaches did not work, which led you to your final solution, including any ablation studies you may want to report, contribute 5% to your total score.

6d. Individual contributions of each of the team members, when clearly written, contribute to 2% of your total score. Please also mention what references you used to come up with your approach - concepts taught in the class, LLMs, papers, etc. You will NOT be penalized for honestly disclosing what helped you ideate.
 
Also, while it is enough for any one member from each team to submit the report for the whole team, please make sure that the report is submitted in <Team name>_report.pdf format, with the report clearly stating the team name and team members' names and roll numbers in the beginning of the report.
